# Feedbox brief — Thursday 19 November 2026

Anthropic went public on Thursday. It [priced at $83 a share][ant-price] and opened at $111.50, raising **$81.3 billion**. The Verge [covered the debut][ant-trade] and reports a first-half net loss of $80.7 billion, mostly an accounting charge, on $16.3 billion of revenue. A [second piece][ant-good] picks at the governance. The Long-Term Benefit Trust names four of seven board seats, but **one of those four needs founder approval**.

Three posts landed on who gets to read your messages. The Investigatory Powers Tribunal has forced the UK to [admit][tcn] it served Apple a secret order under the Investigatory Powers Act. A barrister for Liberty and Privacy International called the secrecy "the emperor's new clothes". ICE [pays AT&T][ice] analysts to sift raw call records, and the subpoena behind it needs **no judge**. The European Commission [finished moving][matrix] more than 40,000 staff onto its own Matrix server in September.

The chat interface was argued both ways on Thursday, and nobody linked anybody. [dbushell.com][dbu] runs a **satire**: a traveller's day dismantled by chatbots that book him as freight. [Jesper Bylund][jb] wants the opposite. Put an agent behind every screen, he argues, and a cancelled flight can be **talked through rather than abandoned**. Canva [added an off switch][canva] for its generative tools.

Wednesday's brief ran Dave Winer's Markdown browser, and the idea has been put to a host. [Writing Slowly][ws] asks Micro.blog's founder to serve .md files beside the HTML, which would suit Winer's readers and anyone wanting **machine-readable pages**. [Mikes Thoughts][mp] gives the lived version: one journal file a day, no database, no CMS, just folders with GitHub doing the rest. **Files first**, he writes.

## Talking points

1. **October was the month the dam broke.** [Eternal October][eo] lists the wave: PHP2Shell exploited in the wild, a backdoored LiteLLM release, eMudhra keys guessable in seconds, and attacks on Korean banks driven by agentic tools. One analysis read that last one as the work of a single person.

2. **Being refused permission to patch your own product is absurd.** A senior engineer [hit Claude Code's safeguards][un] while fixing a security hole in software he owns. The exemption route wants his passport and several days. He finished the job with an EU-hosted open-weights model instead.

3. **The useful part of talking to a machine is what answers back.** Christopher Butler [dictates on dog walks][cb], then has a model read the transcript and argue with him. He trained it to be blunt. It dug up a podcast episode he made in 2018 that had specified this exact setup.

4. **AI is a cheat code, and cheat codes spoil games.** Koen van Gilst [shipped a game][kv] he had never managed to make on his own, then felt flat. His analogy is the key combination that unlocked the whole map in Civilization. "Any game is possible and 99.9999% of them are worthless."
