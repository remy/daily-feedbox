# Feedbox brief — Saturday 31 October 2026

Black October has not run out. [Pixel Envy][pxlnv] quotes Rusty Foster's October roundup. It lists a remote-exploitable race condition in the Linux IPv4 TCP code. Other kernel bugs reached **a public mailing list before their patches were ready**. A compromised Claude Code extension for VS Code shipped a persistent SSH backdoor. The linklog's own reading: this is **a preview, not a coincidence**. Tuesday's brief ran the argument that disclosure volume is itself the attack.

Blocking bots has become a capacity problem. [One WordPress site][narf] fitted two bot traps and went unresponsive. The traps worked; **bots queued ahead of humans** for a fixed set of PHP workers. The fix was a separate PHP-FPM pool for trapped traffic, then Nginx in place of Apache, which queued slow replies one layer up. Separately, [Emu Cafe][emucafe] blocklisted RSSing for republishing its full-text feed for ads. A [third site][mufusa] prefers **a shared crawler blocklist**.

Friday's thread on AI and craft has turned to prose. [One post][lanzani] argues boring, predictable code is often the best code, and LLMs are good at it. Boring prose is merely **mediocre**; its writer reads as a "milquetoast meat proxy". [Baldur Bjarnason][baldur] goes further: writing is thinking, and machine writing is **ash, the burnt embers of dead thoughts**. [A third post][kuhnhardt] holds the other pole, arguing the centaur needs more skill, not less.

Three web-platform items pulled in both directions. [hacdias][xslt] is removing XSLT from his styled RSS feeds after two years, because the three major browser vendors are dropping XSLT 1.0. He notes libxslt **went unmaintained for a stretch of 2025**. Mozilla has an [intent to prototype][gecko] retaining focus when an element becomes disabled, the bug that pushes developers to reinvent `aria-disabled`. Blink is supportive; WebKit has given no signal. One [Interop 2027 shortlist][interop] puts `reading-flow` **ahead of masonry**.

## Talking points

1. **A third of one blogger's outbound links are dead.** [Louis Gray][louisgray] audited nearly 30,000 links across more than 3,000 posts. Just over a third now go nowhere. Pew put the failure rate for decade-old links at nearly 40 per cent, so he is about average. Link rot is the running cost of the open web, and nobody budgets for it.
2. **A breach notice only works if somebody reads it.** Scribd's letters went out on Monday 26 October, naming email address, card last four digits, issuer, expiry and payment history. The [Hacker News post][scribd] that surfaced it opens by saying nothing had been reported anywhere.
3. **Amazon's AI memo says both things on purpose.** [The Register][register] notes that "a finding, not a failing" sits three sentences from a warning that non-adopters are falling behind. A manager can pick whichever reading the moment calls for. That is cover, not confusion.
4. **A coding harness is "curl glued to popen()".** [Prospero][prospero] is a local-model harness in C++ with an ImGui interface, claiming two orders of magnitude less resource use than the web-stack alternatives. Light enough to supervise a fuzz campaign without the OOM killer reaching for the harness first.
