# Feedbox brief — Sunday 1 November 2026

The AI bill arrived at the markets. [Ian Betteridge][betteridge] notes Alphabet fell 12.6% on 29 October, shedding about $500bn, after Google said AI Mode earns less than ordinary search. He also cites Apollo's chief economist, who finds **zero payoff outside tech**. [CNN][cnn] counts $1.6 trillion of AI spend since 2023, then asks how long the bond market will fund it. [Jay Cuthrell][fudge] says lenders are **already tightening on the smaller neoclouds**.

[Senko Rašić][senko] argues that services now police their users. Claude Code drops to an older model when a classifier smells security work. He cites Redis creator Salvatore Sanfilippo and Armin Ronacher hitting the same wall. **A tool you pay for decides what work you may do.** [Interdependent Thoughts][zylstra] answers with a rule of thumb: stop chasing the best model. **Good enough** is an open-weight model within a year of the frontier.

Three posts are pure upkeep, and all three are worth copying. [alexwlchan][archive] now checks his web archive with Playwright, flagging any saved page that still **fetches from the live site**. [splitbrain][tailscale] moved his home services behind Tailscale subnet routing, with the access policy in Git and pushed by a GitHub Action. [Dariusz Winkler][home] rebuilt his site around three **perspectives** — dev, photography, life — each with its own bio.

[Dead Parrot BBS][webrings] makes the case for webrings. The post puts the 1997 service at over 10,000 rings and 80,000 sites. Each ring had a theme and a ringmaster who pruned it. **The narrow scope was the strength**, not the flaw. The idea is being tried again. [Andrew Doran][doran] adds Bubbles to his IndieWeb discovery list, and [Joe Jenett][twofer] points at rsschat, **built from parts of the web and nothing more**.

## Talking points

1. **Proton's problem is management, not AI.** [Raed][raed] can no longer recommend it, and reads the employee defences on Reddit as crisis management. [Troy Hunt][hunt] disliked Yen's language but agreed with part of the manifesto. Friday's brief ran the investigation; this is the split it left behind.

2. **An invisible Pac-Man frightened people in 2010.** Marcin Wichary's [Google doodle][pacman] autoplayed with sound after ten seconds of idling. A Firefox extension called CoolPreviews opened google.com quietly at startup, so the music came from a tab nobody could see.

3. **Groff is faster than your build tool.** A 120-page novella [typesets in 1.6 seconds][groff] on a PC over thirteen years old, from a macro package with three macros. Forty thousand words of it were written in ten days.

4. **Cascade layers deserve an afternoon.** [Kitty Giraudel][layers] put `@layer` on a personal site with no real need for it. The gain is that specificity no longer leaks between buckets.
