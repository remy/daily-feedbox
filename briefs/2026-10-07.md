# Feedbox brief — Wednesday 7 October 2026

Two posts on Wednesday went looking for a number. Both came back doubting it. [One counted][sand] commits that admit AI help across twenty-one language projects: **Julia 53.3%, Ruby 32.4%, GCC 3.1%**. The count only sees commits that say so, so **silence reads as zero**. [The other][residue] walks a worked example. An agent takes 600 easy support cases, output holds at 1,000, and the **bill rises from $7,200 to $7,680**.

[OpenAI put out][openai-math] new maths results on Tuesday, all from a model it has not released. [The Verge counts][verge] 722 papers across 372 groups of results. The average one used about three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking. The firm says it drew on advice from a panel of mathematicians. [One mathematician's reply][slop] quotes the advice back: **stop testing hard problems on closed models**. Tuesday's brief had the Erdős end of this. The labs' end has now landed.

Two more Wednesday posts pick apart Apple's browser chrome. [One found][fantinel] that Safari reads the pixels at the foot of the screen. Those pixels decide whether the floating URL bar goes solid, and it **stays solid once a menu shuts**. The fix is a 6px clear sticky element. [The other traced][aresluna] Safari's tab-drag back through a 2013 camera dial to the 2007 timer wheel. **Apple's own site still has the first bug.**

Wednesday brought its counterweight too. [PhotoCraft][photocraft] is an early-alpha image editor written in Rust, and it opens layered Photoshop files: **134 of 135 test files round-trip byte for byte**. [A 1993 BBS in Mexico][bbs] replays from a capture buffer on a homebrew Z280. [Another site][sounds] put up **over 160 sound-art sketches** as open source. [One blogger's take][baty] on all this: if the app works, he will use it.

## Talking points

1. **Seniors are the ones at risk, not engineers.** [A junior at Criteo][criteo] argues the real danger is a cohort only ever fit to review one agent's work with the help of another. The post lists what the seniors on the team can do: defend code they shipped, argue a design choice rather than just hold one, and spot an agent going the wrong way.

2. **An AI video of the victim has cost a sentence.** [An Arizona appeal court][aivideo] has ordered a new sentencing, because a scripted AI likeness of a manslaughter victim carried "undue emotional weight". The guilty verdict stands; the ten-and-a-half-year term does not. The court weighed it against a 2007 case, where graveside photos of a victim's children nearly crossed the same line.

3. **JPEG XL is back in Chrome, and the decoder is Rust.** [Chrome 155 ships decode support][jxl], with claims of 30–50% better packing than JPEG. The notable part is the rewrite. Image decoders are a prime target, so they shipped a pure-Rust decoder in place of the C++ one.

4. **All 23 of Internet Explorer's page transitions now run on web standards.** [A library][ietrans] reads IE4's old `Page-Enter` meta tag and plays the RevealTrans effects through the View Transition API. Checkerboard dissolves, vertical blinds, random bars — a side project two years in the making.
