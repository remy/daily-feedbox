# Feedbox brief — Monday 9 November 2026

Three writers landed on the same culprit, and it is attention. [I'm the Bottleneck][bottleneck] names it plainly: the limit is the person, not the agent. It lists context switching, decision fatigue and "idle agent anxiety". [Sergey Kuhne][brainfry] answers with a git-backed ticket queue, so **nothing important lives in his head**. [Tedium][yak] quit an LLM-led tablet revival after about fifty "last thing before sound" messages. He calls it **a dopamine loop with no payoff**, and has put the device away.

The crawler tax on small sites turned practical. [shkspr.mobi][banhammer] cut bot traffic by **over 80%** with a honeypot: a hidden link, a robots.txt disallow, and an .htaccess ban written on contact. The hobby site behind it moved more than 2.5TB in a single month. [The Map Room][maproom] pulled its posts from mid-2011 and earlier offline. There, bots outnumbered humans **by an order of magnitude**. Saturday's hardening thread now has numbers behind it.

[Aaron Gustafson][syscolors] found his own component demo's buttons inaccessible on Android in dark mode. Chrome pairs `HighlightText` grey on `Highlight` blue. That is **1.98:1**, failing all five WCAG thresholds. CSS system colours name roles, not hues. So the names promise a pairing the browser does not keep. He has published a System Color Study comparing contrast across browsers and settings. It ends with a request to implementers. Friday's worry about accessibility **"in theory"** just got a test case.

Reading itself came up too. [Read Blogs][readblogs] counts 300-plus subscriptions and almost no rage bait. The claim is structural: blogs are opt-in, so annoying people costs you readers. It ships three OPML starter packs. [smartpeopleiknow.com][fun] points instead at the Tiny Awards shortlist, where the sites are **fun rather than useful**. Neither post argues with the other. Both treat **discovery as the thing worth rebuilding**.

## Talking points

1. **WebMCP cuts the guesswork, and you can measure it.** A TodoMVC demo with seven declared tools took 4 browser actions instead of 13. It read no screenshots at all. [alfy.blog][webmcp] has the numbers and the code.

2. **Webmentions are a consent problem, not an access problem.** [offgridholdout.org][offgrid] picks up the argument Thursday's brief ran, and draws the line at access versus consent. It also runs on Kev Quirk's PureBlog, so his retreat from Fediverse comments lands on its own site.

3. **One blogger is now writing comment policy for the bots.** [ohheybrian.com][shaming] swaps spam comments for a notice saying no human wrote them. He also leaves a hidden instruction in the source, aimed at any agent that reads it.

4. **Nvidia's board is investigating its own founder.** Its [statement][nvboard] says Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, and his [memo to staff][nvmemo] admits the affair. Governance risk in the AI trade now rests on one person's judgement.
