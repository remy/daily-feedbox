# Feedbox brief — Thursday 8 October 2026

The argument over OpenAI's maths release, which ran in Wednesday's brief, got its organised reply. The [Association for Human Mathematics][ahm] asks mathematicians to **stop working with OpenAI** and to centre human understanding again. Releasing over 700 files at once, it says, is **a show of power rather than scholarship**. Terry Tao [reposted the statement][tao] as a guest post. The group adds that OpenAI ignored the advisory body's opening request.

A [Data Drop study][holding] took the full change history of 23 pieces of software that phones and servers depend on. **Eleven of the 23 had one or two people** doing the regular work. Todd Miller made **5,408 of 5,409 changes to sudo** between 2008 and 2018. [Boring technology][boring] wins, a second post argues, because its failure modes are already known. [Notes from an Alexander Petros talk][treadmill] make the same bet on plain HTML.

Dave Winer's "web of Markdown" idea has turned into a working thread. [Andrew Shell][shell] built a proxy that browses sites by their Markdown source. On Thursday [Writing Slowly][slowly] turned it into a feature request for Micro.blog. It asks for an `.md` file per post, a `rel="alternate"` discovery tag and an optional `llms.txt`. What drives it is odd: **agents want plain text**, so **the plain web has a sponsor**.

The Firefox team has reopened its [Interop ranking][rank] for the 2027 proposals. Last year's 1,900-plus rankings shaped what Firefox built next, so it is **a cheap way to vote**. [Oh Hello Ana][ana] posted hers in three lists, splitting fun from client requests. A separate [ACD proposal][acd] makes the sharper point: a green "supported" tick **does not mean accessible**. Its button tests alone pass 300 results.

## Talking points

1. **No whisky for Sam Altman.** Asaf Karagila had promised a bottle for a result on the Partition Principle, then [read the preprint and refused][karagila]. It is muddled and cites unpublished lecture notes, he says, and any journal would reject it on sight. Hundreds of such files are a denial of service on expert attention.

2. **Being called a bot is a false-positive problem.** Told his posts were slop, Stefano [dug out his own paper from 2002][dragas]. The same habits are all there, 24 years early. The paper covered anomaly detection, whose known flaw is a high false-alarm rate.

3. **The Adobe knock-off is real but rough.** [Tedium installed ArtCraft][tedium], the suite that ran in Wednesday's brief, and found Rust binaries of tens rather than hundreds of megabytes. Colour pickers and sub-menus are poor, and DesignCraft missed his fonts until an update shipped minutes earlier.

4. **The footage is worse than the ruling.** 404 Media got the [bodycam video of the Flock search][flock] a federal judge called indiscriminate mass surveillance. A reader had to buy it from the Tulsa court clerk, because the court never put it online. The deputy ran the search while still driving at 60mph.
