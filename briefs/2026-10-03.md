# Feedbox brief — Saturday 3 October 2026

Apple says it will tighten Full Disk Access on the Mac, citing AI agents. Its [developer note][apple-fda], posted on Friday, warns that some apps expose mail, messages and browsing history. This **picks up Tuesday's Muse thread**: Meta says Muse reads Messages only when [both settings are enabled][verge-apple]. Apple gives no date. One user's [own check][fda-blog] found eight apps with the permission, mostly cleaners and launchers.

An OpenAI safety author has quit. David Robinson wrote the safety reports for model launches. [The Verge reports][verge-robinson] that his Atlantic piece wants labs run like **nuclear plants**, with layers of backup. That follows Wednesday's voluntary pledge, which carried no penalties. A [Cambridge paper][casp] with Hinton and Bengio among its authors warns of an **intelligence explosion**. It says automated AI research could speed up fast. It asks policymakers for visibility now.

Aleph Alpha released **Kolibri** on Saturday, an English-German mixture-of-experts model with 78 billion parameters, about 3.5 billion active per token. The weights are open under [Apache 2.0][aleph]. Training ran in Germany and Finland. The company pitches it as sovereign, for government, industry and aerospace. A [write-up][tej] says it beats every model of its size that was compared. That is the company's own test. The author notes a friend worked on it.

Three personal sites show people building their own plumbing. Somebits dropped a 25-year-old Blosxom setup for a [static generator][somebits]. Claude Code finished it in **two days**, after years of a stalled prototype. Vinit Kumar wrote a [Mac RSS reader][rss] with no accounts and no outside dependencies. Matthew Pennell found his [ATproto token][atproto] expired after two hours, so every post since his first write-up **failed without notice**.

## Talking points

1. **A federal judge has said a Flock search broke the law.** Judge Sara Hill ruled on Thursday that a deputy needed a warrant to look up a plate. It sets no binding precedent. It takes Wednesday's Flock thread from data pooling to the courts. [404 Media][flock].
2. **A fake client attacked a developer through a git hook.** A developer was sent a Dropbox folder. Its `.git` directory held a hook that fetched and ran a binary. The lure was an NDA "on a branch". [His account][wiles] is worth passing on.
3. **Age checks now reach personal sites.** The Zarf blog says iOS flags his site as [restricted for some UK users][zarf]. He did not set that rule. Separately, a court [blocked Utah's anti-VPN rules][eff] as a burden on people outside the state.
4. **Git platforms should keep the prompts.** A colleague told the author of [this post][lord] that corrections and failed tries stay on one laptop. Yet they explain choices that would look odd to the next person.
