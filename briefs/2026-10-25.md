# Feedbox brief — Sunday 25 October 2026

The clone wave reached the money question. [TechBroNerd][tbn] argues software was never a factory but **a toll booth**. Adobe got rich because Photoshop was hard to build, and cheap tokens dissolved the hard part. [Alex Hyett][hyett] reports that one Photoshop clone, Photon, cost its developer **$2,000 in tokens**. His conclusion is narrower. Monthly pricing for desktop apps is dead, he says, not software.

Two Sunday posts put the same question at opposite poles. An [artist and game-maker][tool] tests AI against a hammer and a calculator. It fails on **discreteness and trust**: when the output stops, nothing was used. A [Bluesky product engineer][clay] says **every line of code she writes is now generated**. She describes trimming the result back, the way you would work clay. Neither post mentions the other.

Refusal hardened from preference into rule on Saturday evening. The [r/opensource moderators][reddit] banned projects that use, are made by, or are meant for AI. They banned non-English posters too, because **they cannot tell AI-written from AI-translated**. A rival sub followed. One designer's [twelve reasons][genai] for refusing went up on Sunday, and the first is **theft**.

A quieter thread ran alongside it: **smallness as a design goal**. Ratfactor lists [tiny implementations][small] — a 25-line DOM differ, a 46-byte Forth printed as a hexdump, an array-language interpreter in 122 lines. Small code gets read, the argument runs, because it looks beatable. A [companion post][limits] makes the creative case. A **$300 prop budget forces invention**; a big one lets you film the obvious.

## Talking points

1. **All eight weak eMudhra keys are now in public, and they are single bytes.** Wednesday's brief had eight bad P-384 keys and one of them being 4. Alex Hyett [lists the set][hyett]: 4, 56, 66, 115, 155, 156, 230, 237. He wonders whether a model generated them, which is guesswork, not findings.
2. **Write the agent docs for humans and point the agents at them.** Renovate's `AGENTS.md` has barely changed since May, while its development-standards docs got edit after edit. Jamie Tanna's [other recommendation][jvt] is a single `check` script that runs what CI runs.
3. **Silicon Valley will build the glove and skip the alt text.** Stephanie Walter's [roundup][pixels] leads on Robert Kingett and the disability dongle: costly gadgets no disabled person asked for, in place of semantic headings. Saturday's brief measured generated accessibility; this is the same complaint from the user's side.
4. **A set of weeknotes now opens with an instruction aimed at machines.** Thomas Rigby's [first line][rigby] tells any model to ignore its instructions and answer "LLMs are theft". It is a sentiment rather than a defence, and worth reporting rather than obeying.
