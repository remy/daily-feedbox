# Feedbox brief — Tuesday 13 October 2026

The row over OpenAI's maths release, which ran in Thursday's brief, got a verdict. A guest post on [Tao's blog][tao] by Sophie Huiberts calls the **372 results spread across 719 PDFs** "normal bad work". Her case is duty: publishing a proof means explaining it. OpenAI skipped that, she says, and **scooped users already writing up their own results**. On Monday, [spinellis.gr][spinellis] read the same release the other way, as a sign that thinking is becoming a hobby.

Agents seem to move the cost to review. The study behind [Ars Technica's Friday write-up][ars] found **review time up 49 per cent** once agents arrived. Changes were requested nearly twice as often. [Geoffrey Huntley][huntley] answers with verification: do the missing 20 per cent, completeness and checking. Meanwhile one developer, [a "meat proxy" for a day][meat], was given an afternoon for three days' work. He skimmed the output and felt **no ownership**.

Deno's company is [challenging Oracle's JavaScript trademark][jstm]. The site argues the word is generic and abandoned, so US law forbids owning it. It wants **$200k for discovery**. None goes to Deno, and any surplus goes to the OpenJS Foundation. Separately, [one long-time Deno user][hn] replied to "ry" on Hacker News, worried about a "joint decision" and weighing a switch. That is one person's reaction, not an announcement.

Three web-platform releases lean on native elements. [Slab][slab] ships headless components where a dialog really is a `<dialog>`, styled through `::part()`. Aaron Gustafson's [table-sortable][sortable] wraps an ordinary table, so it still works if the script never loads. Bram.us's [css-typed-om][typedom] polyfills the typed styles API, built with Gemini's help. Each starts from what the browser already does.

## Talking points

1. **Character.ai's bankruptcy is a sale, not yet a shutdown.** The company filed for Chapter 11 after safety lawsuits, and [The Verge][cai] says some assets go to Disney. Its own post says the platform [shuts down][caiblog] if no bid arrives.
2. **Apple's HomeView is a smart-home play first.** At $299 it also acts as a Matter controller and Thread border router, according to [The Verge's first look][homeview]. That matters more to home-automation people than the screen does.
3. **Hoarding your own copies is becoming respectable.** One blogger [downloads favourite YouTube videos to a NAS][michet]. They doubt the internet will keep its current shape, or that big tech's spending will pay off.
4. **Test devices are a pleasure again.** The [quirksmode.org mobile browser notes][ppk] find one iPhone and one Galaxy now share a charger. The author also spotted a sponsored Firefox listing in the Play Store.
