# Feedbox brief — Wednesday 11 November 2026

Tuesday's brief carried Chrome's Modern Web Guidance 1.0. [Chrome][chrome] has now put numbers behind it. Top coding agents pass about **52% of its eval suite** on their own. With the guidance loaded, they pass **91%**. The misses are basic: keyboard focus, `inert`, reduced motion, form hints. [AWS][aws] came at the same gap from the other side. Its new emulator gives agents a local copy of 200-odd services to test against. Agents wrote all of it.

Accessibility ran on from Friday's WCAG 3 thread, this time as duty and as tooling. [TetraLogical][tetra] reads the EU AI Act: from **December 2027**, makers of high-risk AI must meet EN 301 549 and WCAG 2.2. Aaron Gustafson's team [open-sourced a toolkit][act] that agents can read as YAML. [Eric Eggert][yatil] is less cheerful. Disabled people now patch their own access with LLMs, and he calls the cost a **disability tax**.

Tuesday's brief covered surveillance people buy for their own homes. [The Verge][tvs] went at the makers instead. It put **17 yes-or-no questions** to eleven TV and streaming firms. Hisense never replied, after five tries. Sony, Panasonic and Vizio skipped the ones they did not like. [Mozilla][moz] went for measuring instead. Its study of fingerprinting drew on **nearly 25 million Firefox users**. The paper is public.

Tuesday's small-tools argument got an answer by action. Kevin shipped [Paper Reader][paper] as a standalone alpha, so FreshRSS is no longer needed. Elsewhere the question was simpler: who is the writing for? [Olu Online][olu] would rather the words were **stolen in their prime** than never written. [Rach Smith][rach] is back after a year away. She now gets **cold emails from agents**, asking to be taught how to build a unique site.

## Talking points

1. **Reckless vibe-coding is as bad as pushing untested code to main.** Swiftrocks writes about people who prompt an agent and forward the output unread. Code review will not catch it, because the reviews are farmed out too. [Reckless vibe-coding][vibe]

2. **Tim Peters has died, and Python has lost a quiet shaper of the language.** Guido van Rossum broke the news on Wednesday; Peters died on 15 July. He wrote the Zen of Python and Timsort, and Java and Android both took that up. [The thread][peters]

3. **The EU wants an off switch for addictive design.** The draft Digital Fairness Act covers loot boxes, endless scroll, autoplay and AI companions. Games bodies call it "unworkable". [Digital Fairness Act][dfa]

4. **Scroll-driven animations took twelve years to land everywhere.** Firefox 159 closed the set in October, so the feature is now Baseline newly available. Apple made the first pitch in 2014. [The timeline][sda]
