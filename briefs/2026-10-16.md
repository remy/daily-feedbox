# Feedbox brief — Friday 16 October 2026

The review bottleneck ran on from Tuesday, now with a shipped release behind it. [PostgreSQL 19 has slipped to around 19 November][pg19], and eight to ten features were pulled. Committer Peter Eisentraut points at **LLM-assisted code review**, which surfaced a long tail of edge cases early. "You're staring at a list of forty defect reports," he wrote. Each needs a small fix, but the overhead adds up. He is clear that **this was not vibe coding**.

The Lean argument ran on from Tuesday, now answered by the formalisers. Seven of them have replied to the call to formalise all mathematics quickly. [Their joint post][xena] is sympathetic but wary. **A poor formalisation is worse than none**, they argue, because nobody will use it. Anthropic's 13 million lines on Fermat's Last Theorem impressed them and changed little. Mathlib has 950 contributors; the AI-written Tau Ceti is already **three million lines**.

One post answering another by name, which is rare. JP at Modded Bear [argues things are still worth doing yourself][mb], prompted by Epic Games going all in on AI. Off Grid Holdout [names the post and disagrees][ogh]: troubleshooting a kernel module with AI **is still troubleshooting**. Epic's line that **programming is hard** also drew [a flat rejection][prog] from an eleven-year veteran.

Chromium's upkeep rests on fewer people than its reach suggests. Sharon Yang [moved from Google to Igalia][igalia] and works in the same repository. Igalia has about 180 staff against Google's 180,000, and is **the largest contributor that does not make a browser**. Meanwhile Gentoo has [given its Chromium package last rites][gentoo]. Sam James calls it the package that burns through maintainers the most. A bug report counted **602 known vulnerabilities** in the stale ebuild.

## Talking points

1. **Deno's runtime is ending, and this time it is official.** Ryan Dahl's own announcement promises a year of bug fixes and security updates, then an end to development. Tuesday's brief would not call it a shutdown on one user's word. That caution can now go. [Dave Bushell has the quote][deno].

2. **WordPress reverted a six-year accessibility fix after one comment.** Mullenweg told Andrea Fercia, a committer on 33 releases, to contribute his talents elsewhere, and gave no reason. Joe Dolson reverted 19 hours later, citing the complexity of admin notices. A counting bug was still open at the time. Both things are true, and only one is a technical argument. [The Repository has the timeline][repo].

3. **Meta's new commercial terms take effect on 30 October.** Three lawyers told Nieman Lab the wording could cover scraping news sites to train AI. Short of deleting the business account, there is no opt-out. A Meta business page becomes a standing grant, whichever robots file you ship. [Read the analysis][nieman].

4. **Put your Interop 2027 priorities in order while the form is open.** Jeremy Keith puts web app installation first, then speculation rules. He links eight other people's lists too. Styleable `select` is the common favourite, and that is the signal browsers will act on. [See his five][adactio].
