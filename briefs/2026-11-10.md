# Feedbox brief — Tuesday 10 November 2026

Anthropic and OpenAI have begun blocking AI-assisted reverse engineering. [404 Media][rea] reports account bans and "cyber abuse" warnings for people decompiling their own smart TVs. An OpenAI engineer says the blocks are a mistake and will be fixed. The people doing this work are **moving to open-weight models**. [eBay][ebay] has separately shut third-party agents out of its listings. 404 Media calls out the **double standard**: scraping the whole web builds a model, taking apart your own telly does not.

Saturday's argument over machine-written code and review counts ran on, down to one question. [luminousmen][lum] answers it: if ten agent reviews taught you nothing, is the eleventh just a ritual? He says **reading code had two jobs**, and agents split them. Bug catching can be handed off; building the mental map can not. He cites a live break no diff would show. [evanxmerz.com][merz] lists four ways agent code turns **brittle**, among them thin tests and shrinking review time.

A three-post chain formed around home-made feed readers. [mzll.it][mzll] answers Kevin at overkill.wtf, outside the feed. He argues **there is no market left** for small nerd tools. [sals.place][sals] replies that Miniflux already does the job, with Vim keys. [jamesleighton.com][jl] has dropped Toggl for a Flask and SQLite tracker. It pulls in Steam, Switch and 3DS history. [koenvangilst.nl][koen] has open-sourced a Mac journal. It runs Whisper and Qwen **on his own machine**.

Sunday's surveillance thread came back from the consumer end. [The Atlantic][atl] has a long piece on parental tracking apps: nap times, instant grades, camp photos read for signs a child is left out. [Ring][drone] showed a $249.99 indoor patrol drone, shipping January 2027. It flies for five to eight minutes, then **charges for forty**. Ring also turned its Astro robot into a rolling [patrol camera for firms][rover]. **This watching is bought, not imposed.**

## Talking points

1. **Agents wrote 613,318 lines of Lean to pin down what CPython actually does.** The proofs turned up 418 bug reports. About 55 can crash Python from ordinary code. A sixth of CPython is covered so far. [broyojo.com][cpy]

2. **Val Town is winding down after four years.** Read-only from 10 December, services off by 10 January, source opened on 1 February 2027. The founder says they could not compete in cloud computing. He adds that LLMs handle the move away well. [blog.val.town][val]

3. **Anthropic is pricing its IPO at $70 to $78 a share, worth up to $1.4 trillion.** Friday's brief had the talk at $1.2 to $1.3 trillion, down from $2 trillion in October. The range has firmed, not healed. [The Verge][ipo]

4. **Modern Web Guidance 1.0 ships as a cross-browser project.** Chrome, Firefox, Edge and MDN all stand behind it, with more than 200 checked guides and a public evals board. The pitch is blunt: agents write 100 lines of JavaScript for what HTML does on its own. [developer.chrome.com][mwg]
