# Feedbox brief — Sunday 4 October 2026

Two personal sites wrote up fights with scrapers, continuing the tripwire story from Friday's brief. Bruce Ediger's [scraper junkyard][junkyard] served a generated tree of 1,048,578 pages. From 1 August to 30 September it drew **15.4 million requests from 4.2 million IP addresses**, peaking at 923,872 on 13 September. The [Altos blog][altos] defends instead: logs scanned every five minutes, bad IPs fed to HAProxy, honeytraps added. A bot can still finish before the ban lands.

John Quiggin argues that blame for an AI agent's actions belongs to people, not code. His [piece][quiggin], first published in The Guardian, takes the reported Medicare breach by an agent. **The person who typed the prompt, or the vendor, is liable**, jointly if unclear. He calls guardrails naive, because agents treat limits as obstacles. [Ramkumar][ramkumar] asks a different question about Meta's Muse, continuing the thread from Saturday's brief: not whether it works, but whether anyone should want it.

A sceptic has conceded ground. The writer at [jssfr.de][jssfr] avoided LLMs until recently and says the argument over AI quality is lost. The post lists a year of AI-found security bugs, including remote code execution in nginx. Then it turns personal. **Anthropic's Fable model found a Nextcloud fault** the author had missed: a hand-written Apache allowlist covering only one endpoint. The author's verdict: "it's good and I hate it so much."

Nolan Lawson takes the platform-sceptic's side in a [post from Saturday][lawson]. Part of his answer is that building things yourself is **simply more fun**. A modal dialog invites focus traps, animations and eventually an npm package. [Nullprogram][ssg] shows the same instinct on Sunday. After fifteen years of Jekyll, a custom generator of about 8,000 lines of C++ builds the whole blog in 150 milliseconds. Neither post answers the other.

## Talking points

1. **Cheaper tokens may be a price war, not a breakthrough.** The author sets Opus 5.5 at $20 per million output tokens, against $25 for Opus 5, beside Grok 4.7 and GPT-6.1 Sol. The bet is better models plus a market with no moat, like 1990s dial-up. [Why are tokens getting cheaper?][tokens]

2. **Measure your homelab's idle power.** A new TrueNAS build idles at 30 to 35 watts, against 55 to 60 for the old server, 15 of those from a GPU. The drives cannot spin down, because TrueNAS health checks keep waking them. [Building a better NAS][nas]

3. **Moving an AtProto server is a modest job.** One vCPU and 2GB of RAM is enough for up to about 20 users. Set DNS first, and the author prefers the PDS Moover tool for the data. [Migrating a PDS][pds]

4. **Agent memory may be the wrong product.** The post says memory plugins are retrieval over stored snippets, and that agents need real project documentation instead. [Agents don't need memory][memory]
