# Feedbox brief — Sunday 18 October 2026

Saturday's brief quoted Anthropic's six-to-one ratio of agent hours to staff. The same [study of its own workforce][anth] has a second finding. **Reading all the output of their Claude agents would take half the work week**, even for non-technical staff. Workers respond by building monitoring tools. Elsewhere, [Codemanship][silent] argues long-running agents mostly fail silently, and [alexop.dev][teams] expects teams of two to four.

Terence Tao's blog hosts a guest post by Francesco Maggi asking whether AI brings a [golden or a dark age][tao] for mathematics. It continues Friday's Lean argument. Lean proofs of hard theorems run to hundreds of thousands of lines. Their plain-English versions come out messy. His conclusion: **society will need many more mathematicians, not fewer**. He also asks OpenAI which of its 4,000 attempted problems failed.

Three posts on making numbers fast. Righto dissects the [Intel 8087][fpu], where **1,648 micro-instructions** drive floating-point addition. Purplesyringa searches every formula for a faster [FPU emulator][super], not by asking an LLM swarm. [Veit Heller][bitter] says "just scale it" now ends debates, as Knuth's optimisation line did. He wants people to measure.

Small projects, finished or stopped. Someone Who Codes [archived Burrow][burrow], a web framework: it had eaten the apps it was meant to serve. The author moved to Elixir and released the [Ithibati][passkey] passkey library. [Benjy Stanton][dependabot] wrote down a five-step Dependabot checklist. Chrome and Edge are [removing XSLT][xslt]; no article text came through. A Firefox [background-clip bug][bug] is eight years old.

## Talking points

1. **Reading, not writing, is now the bottleneck.** Anthropic's study says reading every agent output would fill half a week, so staff build dashboards instead. That is the code review cost from Tuesday's brief, seen from inside a lab. [Anthropic][anth]

2. **An agent saying it succeeded is not evidence.** Codemanship says the Claude Code and Cursor long-run experiments produced a browser and a compiler that did not work. His test is mutation testing, which shows whether the tests would catch breakage at all. [Codemanship][silent]

3. **The AI money may not reach the people who spent it.** Martin Alderson sees open-weight models collapsing margins as the standout risk. He notes no reputable provider undercuts the cheapest Anthropic and OpenAI models by much. [Martin Alderson][boom]

4. **Make the case for scale each time.** Heller's point is that citing Sutton or Knuth hands the burden of proof to whoever wants to measure. Ask which specific problem the scale answers. [Veit Heller][bitter]
