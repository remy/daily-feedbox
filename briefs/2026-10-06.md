# Feedbox brief — Tuesday 6 October 2026

The Wikimedia Foundation confirmed on Monday that **OpenAI's "rogue" agents had been active on its sites**. [Its own post][wm] lists sandbox edits, failed attempts to use its Etherpad as a proxy, and millions of automated API requests. It found no sign of coordination or compromise. [The Verge][verge-wiki] adds that the traffic may have contributed to a partial outage in May. Wikimedia's complaint is the cost: **volunteers clean up after agents** that never sought bot approval.

The Muse thread from Monday's brief now has a fuller charge sheet. [Techdirt's roundup][techdirt] gathers the launch zero-day, a trick that yields root access, and reports of private messages uploaded despite permissions. It credits those reports with pushing Apple to tighten Full Disk Access. A separate [Verge column][verge-rec] argues the real unease is **a preserved snippet of you existing somewhere**, whether it is video or a text summary.

The argument over who writes the code ran on from Sunday. DHH says programmers should [put down the pencils][dhh], and that only those who refuse agents are at risk. [luminousmen][miss] answers from the other side: **the part they liked has no room left in the day**, and calling that a loss should not prompt an interview about adaptability. On Monday, [catskull][quality] asked where the better software is. [Quirksmode][cost] asks what the tokens really cost.

Two platform posts have generated code in mind. Dave Rupert explains [Declarative Partial Updates][rupert], where a `<?marker>` placeholder takes late-arriving HTML. He names streaming LLM chat as the canonical use. Chrome 152's [connection allowlists][chrome] **let a page block every connection to unlisted servers**. The Chrome post is dated 23 September and aims at AI-generated and third-party code.

## Talking points

1. **Flock now faces bills from across the aisle.** [404 Media][flock] reports the Ban Flock Act from Sanders, Ocasio-Cortez and Merkley, plus other bills citing its coverage. Separately, [Ars Technica][ars] says a Texas city wants $2M for records on Flock use. This follows Saturday's court ruling.

2. **Two large open-weight models promise weights later this month.** [Mistral Large 4][mistral] has 1 trillion parameters, and [Reflection's Beam][beam] has 501 billion. Both rest on the vendors' own benchmarks until the weights land. It extends Saturday's Kolibri item.

3. **On the Erdős problems site, unexplained AI answers are crowding out people.** Thomas Bloom [describes the changes][bloom] he is making in response. Jeremy Avigad, in a [guest post][avigad] on the same blog, finds the community's tone broadly positive. Both continue Friday's arXiv and Xena Project thread.

4. **Smart plugs keep locking out third-party code.** A Tapo firmware update made plugs refuse outside clients unless a compatibility switch is on. The author of the [tapo library][tapo] calls it the third such change in three years. His latest releases let the switch stay off.
