# Feedbox brief — Saturday 17 October 2026

Friday's code review argument now has the operator's own account. The [Claude Code team][review] set out its flow on Thursday. Merged pull requests more than doubled from June to September. **Reverts fell over 40 per cent.** Most of the reading moved to machines. One pull request ran more than fifty rounds of review in a night. About **59 per cent of what it flagged was pre-existing code**. After three rounds the reviewer now tells the author to rethink.

Two posts treat an agent's prose as a working condition rather than a cosmetic. [Build.ms][calm] wrote a `REVIEW.md` to stop Claude narrating. The walls of text became **"No actionable findings in this PR"**. [Tom Ashworth][comms] went further and moved his work to Codex. He says the deterioration in Claude's communication **affected his mental health**, and that colleagues reported the same.

Surveillance became measurable in two directions. Court papers in a Canadian union case describe Atlas, an Amazon forecasting tool. It **scores warehouses from 100 to 900 for union risk**, drawing on Reddit posts and staff surveys. Sixteen sites sit in the highest-risk tier, two of them in the UK. Amazon says the scoring is at building level only. [MapQuest][flock] added a layer showing **where number plate cameras are**, continuing the Flock thread from the other side.

[Aaron Parecki][indieweb] explained why Claude Code keeps appearing in his IndieWeb commits. The principles are about **owning your domain and your data**, he argues, not about which tools typed the code. His webmention.io moved from Ruby to PHP and nobody using it had to change anything. A rival service, weepinbell, has already appeared in response. [Henrique Dias][mcp], adding MCP to his own site, found MCP's recommended client auth is **essentially IndieAuth**.

## Talking points

1. **The agent pitch depends on access nobody ever promised.** Delta, JPMorgan Chase and Charles Schwab are blocking them outright. Yelp admits only agents that pay for data licensing, and Amazon told Meta's Muse its access breaks site policy. An assistant that cannot reach the shops is a much thinner product. [The Verge][blocked]

2. **Meta pulled its scraping clause once lawyers read it aloud.** The wording suggested a right to use content from any site for training. Meta now says it never changed anything and that robots.txt still governs. The clause went in quietly; the climbdown did not. [The Verge][metaterms]

3. **The firm best placed to measure the junior squeeze is also causing it.** Anthropic's own research reports agent hours outnumbering employees six to one by August, alongside a smaller share of junior technical hires. [Eshu Marneedi][cs] argues judgement is learnt on the job, not screened for at the door.

4. **GDPR works as an export button.** Authy offers no way to get your tokens out. A developer filed an Article 15 and 20 request with Twilio, recovered 33 TOTP seeds, and built his own app to hold them. [Blogccasion][gdpr] credits Authy with handling the request promptly.
