# Feedbox brief — Thursday 12 November 2026

[Nvidia's board][nvidia] confirmed on Thursday that Jensen Huang has resigned, **effective immediately**. Colette Kress, chief financial officer since 2013, becomes interim chief executive. [The Verge][verge-scandal] treats the second affair as a pretext. Its argument is that the **real exposure is Megaspeed**, the Singapore buyer tied to chip-diversion claims. Singapore says the United States gave it little help with its investigation, [the New York Times reports][nyt-singapore]. One [blog post][anani] reads it differently: tycoons are interchangeable, and boards can prove it.

The EU accessibility thread from Wednesday's brief returned with a year of enforcement behind it. A French court gave Carrefour six months to conform, with a daily penalty after that. Its 71% score against France's national standard did not serve as a defence. **The test is whether people can use the site**, not whether the company tried. [TetraLogical][tetra] notes the ruling covered the native app as well, a first. The Act now references **WCAG 2.2 rather than 2.1**.

Tuesday's brief ran the thread on agents being locked out. [United Airlines][united] joined on Wednesday, barring third-party AI agents from bookings over shared credentials and check-in rules. Two OpenAI engineers have taken a draft to the IETF's HTTP working group. It would let an agent forward a user's country, masked IP, locale and time zone. [Joost de Valk][joost] reads it as **a route past those blocks**, and notes that signing the signals stays optional.

Sunday's brief ran the thread on developers refusing LLMs in their own work. It has **hardened into policy**. [Luke Plant][slop] has published a page he points to when he closes a pull request as slop. He says detailed feedback no longer pays. A group of Independent Games Festival jurors will not vote for any game that used generative AI, for assets or code. The studio behind Big Walk then [conceded][coyote] **a fraction of a per cent** of LLM-touched code.

## Talking points

1. **The released Yang-Mills proof does not clear the bar the press release implied.** The Lean code went up on Tuesday. The Clay problem statement rules out weak existence unless the mass gap is also established, [Peter Woit writes][woit].
2. **Deduplicating CSS buys maintainability, not bytes.** CSS Dedup was run over 1,882 style sheets from 123 sites. Raw size fell by one to two per cent. After Gzip or Brotli the saving was almost nothing, [the results show][meiert].
3. **AI crawlers are 2004 Googlebot, and that is worth defending.** They fetch HTML and mostly skip JavaScript. That restores a discipline the web lost when Googlebot began rendering pages in 2014, [this post argues][shiflett]. OpenAI's crawlers have reportedly begun running scripts, though.
4. **Copilot made Okta's engineers happier without moving throughput.** A CACM paper by Fei Liu and Todd McKinnon found no statistically significant change in output metrics, [as noted here][okta]. The data is from 2024, a fair caveat.
