# Feedbox brief — Friday 13 November 2026

[The Register][reg] puts the start of Microsoft's Exchange Online failure at 1826 GMT on Thursday. It ran close to two days, taking Outlook, Teams, SharePoint and Copilot with it. [The Verge][verge-outage] links it to **Azure VNet config changes**. Rivals supplied the lesson: **one vendor is one point of failure**. Dame Chi Onwurah, chair of the Commons Science and Technology Committee, called it a warning on UK digital resilience. [One blogger][jsw] watched workers treat it as a snow day for adults.

Impersonation turned concrete on Friday. [Dave Smyth][smyth] argues that **a site which looks AI-generated is easier to fake**, because the forger uses the same tools and leaves the same tells. Another writer found [his own blog rehosted][mattbee] at mataroa.site, with the stolen images watermarked with his real domain. **Cloudflare's abuse form returned a blank page.** In [The Atlantic][atlantic], the novelist Rachel Kadish met a man objecting to a plot her novel does not contain. She traces it to ChatGPT.

Monday's brief named Bubbles among the discovery tools. Its author has now [cut comments][bubbles] and linked posts that answer each other instead. That removed **over 4,000 lines of code**, and about 19 in 20 of the links are right. [Dale Mellor][mellor] replied on his own site, arguing the indie web needs one agreed comment mechanism rather than five. [Michael Mitchell][feeds] is bleaker: RSSLY found **a working feed on only 24%** of the sites it checked.

[Elliot Jay Stocks][ejs] spent six months and real money testing nearly every Spotify rival. He settled on Qobuz, which is family-run, French, and **pays artists four times the industry average**. He still thinks Spotify is the better product. The [Qobuz Beta theme][hicks] on hicks.design got another update on the same day. Its author's complaint sits at the other end of the stack: **Tailwind leaves too few semantic classes** to restyle the app well.

## Talking points

1. **Browser support is not accessibility support.** [ACD][acd] pairs browsers with screen readers and records what actually works, sitting next to BCD and Baseline. The published table is an illustration rather than real data, but the gap it names is real.
2. **The no-AI line is being written down.** [Open Source Says No][oss] lists projects that ban LLM contributions, among them yt-dlp and Immich. The [No AI Slop manifesto][slop], from an AI engineering summit, refuses purism: its authors hold that unreviewed output is not always bad.
3. **Nvidia staff want Huang back.** An internal suggestion-box post drew more than 1,600 comments, several asking the board to undo the exit, [WIRED][wired] reports. Trump used Truth Social to nominate [Tim Cook][cook]. Thursday's brief ran the resignation; this is the succession fight opening.
4. **A 145B model on a laptop.** [Edge0][edge0] has open-sourced Edge0-145B with weights and an inference stack. It claims 29GB of peak memory and over 80 tokens a second while decoding. Those are the numbers to test first.
