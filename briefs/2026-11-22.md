# Feedbox brief — Sunday 22 November 2026

Dragos sent nearly two million prompts through the AI reseller chain. Each carried a unique secret: an AWS key, a password, a private URL. [Ars Technica][ars] reports three providers were breached outright. Some stolen keys were tested within hours. **Some of the stolen requests were flagged Zero Data Retention.** [OpenRouter][openrouter] answered on Sunday. It off-boarded three providers and **stripped 125 endpoints of ZDR eligibility**.

Saturday's brief ran the two rival GRH proofs. The argument has now turned to method. [Eshu Marneedi][marneedi] carries the New York Times report and a quote from Terence Tao. Tao calls the result **strip mining the landscape**: all the fruit, barren ground after. [Grey Enlightenment][grey] wants a Methods section — models used, split of labour, chat logs, usage tier. Neither disputes the proof. Both want the working shown.

A post on [lethain.com][lethain] reports 115 pull requests shipped in one week. It lists what had to be fixed first: deployment, then **wrong context in the knowledge base**, then issue tracking, then orchestration. [Karl Koch][koch] gets to the same place from the design side at DuckDuckGo. As engineering speeds up, **the handoffs between stages become the work**. [Alex Hyett][hyett] puts a cost on the other side. He lists six November outages and blames thinned-out teams.

Three posts on self-hosting. [James Leighton][leighton] built music-tracker before **the Last.fm API paywall lands on 1 December**. It keeps scrobbles in SQLite and speaks the old API. [Taking SSH off the internet][zsec] shuts port 22 and leaves only 443, reachable from Cloudflare alone. The trigger was an OpenSSH bug on musl, where a long password matches a disabled account. [Jan van den Berg][j11g] runs three servers that back each other up. One is a **€27 thin client**.

## Talking points

1. **You cannot outsource accountability.** [Troy Hunt][hunt] notes that breach notices now say the incident was "at a third party", as if that shifts the blame. It does not. The firm you gave your data to still decides whether to tell you. In the Optimizely case it has not even named the third party.
2. **An approved app is the new stolen password.** [Chris Coyier][coyier] lost his X account without typing a credential. A plausible DM, a real booking page, then a redirect back to X to approve an app with far too many permissions. The warning he clicked past was "username changed recently".
3. **The moat is your data, not the harness.** [James Jarvis][jarvis] argues model quality and harnesses are both becoming commodities. So he put his own health, location and money data in SQLite behind a single `query` tool. The model writes its own SQL, which beats keeping a tool per purpose.
4. **Neither button book covers loading states.** [Unsung][unsung] reviews two guides to interface parts: 250 pages against 812. The short one wins on opinions and pictures. The long one reads as a survey with blurry screenshots. Both skip the state users stare at most.
